I'm a linguist and early career researcher currently based in New Delhi, India.

I am curious about all observable linguistic phenomena that make spoken languages interesting.

My academic interests have so far spanned across two kinds of questions. One is of the theoretical kind, concerning assumptions upon which linguistic theories build claims about the workings of particular language grammars.

Following this theoretical question in my doctoral thesis, I looked at the nature of word formation in Malayalam, and the larger questions it opens up for the domain of Morphology within linguistic theory.

Apart from my work in generative morphology, I'm also intrigued by questions concerning language-use in society, including (but not limited to):

  • What are the ways in which linguistic behavior reflects the attitudes, identities and ideologies of its speakers?
  • What are the social meanings indexed in a given linguistic code?
  • What are the pedagogical principles underlying language education in a multilingual environment?

I have also had a few brief forays outside of academia as a communications consultant, a language policy researcher and a learning facilitator for an independent education collective.

I speak English, Tamil, Malayalam and Hindi (and smatterings of Bangla through eavesdropping on my Bengali friends).

Research

In my doctoral research, I looked at morphology as an autonomous rule-based module as opposed to viewing morphology as a derivative to syntax. Drawing on data from roots in Malayalam, I argued for morphology as a separate domain of linguistic operation within the grammatical architecture albeit with a principled mapping between the syntactico-semantic and the linear-segmental levels of word grammar.

Email me for a copy of my PhD dissertation.

Policy advocacy and communications

Towards the end of my ABD scholar stage, I put away my theoretician cap for a bit and explored the social impact sector drawing on my transferrable skills such as research, writing and communications. I worked for close to three years as a policy and advocacy professional for an international public health firm, liaising closely with government partners and multilateral agencies. My past work includes co-authoring a handbook for social and behavioural change communication strategy for a flagship government program in India.

Language policy and education

I worked as a research consultant with the Language and Learning Foundation, a not-for-profit organization working to improve foundational learning in India, to design and lead a qualitative study to record the knowledge, attitudes and classroom practices of early grade teachers and their pedagogical adaptation of mother tongue based literacy and numeracy education.
